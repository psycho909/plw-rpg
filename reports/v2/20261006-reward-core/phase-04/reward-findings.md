## §61 question 1: did procedural loot change a subsequent action?

**Observed within the agent and integrated stress sequences: yes, locally; causal influence on later target choice remains unproven.** The Adventure trace records 26 visible-loot events and subsequent comparison/equip or keep decisions. The Common spear showed +5 attack against an empty slot; later drops included Bleeding short swords, Blocking/Sturdy armor and boss-exclusive Moon Fang Spears. It logged 706 EQUIP actions, concentrated on repeated instance IDs because the helper applies an automatic positive-stat policy. This is runner churn, not 706 player build changes. In the true final save, `reward.equipped.alden` contains Epic Moon Fang Spear item-16 and Uncommon Warding armor item-14; nulls in `characters[].equipment` are the legacy fixed slots, not missing procedural gear.

The integrated stress artifact independently shows a fresh-save path through fight → visible loot → inspect/compare → equip → higher wolf ranks → Wolf King reward and seven reloads. These ordered observations establish that the app and helper can traverse the progression. They do not establish that the gear change caused a harder-target choice, because the helper follows readable goals and there is no counterfactual or human-choice control. Simulations independently establish tested profile outcome differences: in Early, Epic won 65/96 versus legacy 62/96, Rare 52/96, Defense 51/96, and the sampled actual boss award 67/96. At Edge/Ready most profiles won all 96, with turn/damage/potion outcomes still differing. The isolated +1 piercing-vs-bleeding comparison favored penetration in 12/96, 23/96 and 16/96 paired cells across Early/Edge/Ready, while 77/96, 73/96 and 80/96 were exactly equivalent. These are controlled engine outcomes, not a causal estimate of an actual equip action.

## §61 question 2: is elite/Boss risk worth seeking?

**The reward ladder is visible and reachable in the corrected agent run; human preference/value is not established.** The 30-minute corrected retry records explicit `explicitAdventureGoal` text and `eligibleTargetOptions` on every TRACK. Its first ordered sequence is gray at 0.70s, scarred at 4.95s, elite alpha at 8.42s, pack leader at 41.91s and Wolf King at 85.30s; first goal strings name each selected target, and the eligible lists grow through the corresponding ranks. Overall it made 18 alpha, six Wolf King, and one each gray, scarred and pack-leader selections. Repeated goal text later asked for higher-quality gear/affix comparison or Wolf King-exclusive gear/variant. All five rank definitions were defeated and the boss collection recorded. That supports a normal-save scripted progression path, not human willingness to take the risk.

After targets depleted, the same run recorded 734 legal-rest decisions. Its final blocked sample at population 0.51 showed all five target rows disabled with `附近暫時沒有怪物`; this is observed population pacing, distinct from policy-selected repetition. Combat fixtures show progression-sensitive risk/reward: Early actual boss profile won 2/16 well-fed, 10/16 starved and 7/16 moonlit fights; Edge/Ready won 16/16 in each variant. A matched canonical exclusive base dominated its standard-body counterfactual in 49/96 Early, 46/96 Edge and 45/96 Ready cells; other cells were equivalent. These controlled outcomes show sampled mechanical value and risk, not access frequency, acquisition probability or player willingness.

The original Adventure attempt is policy-confounded by a confirmed QA defect. TRACK #4 at 40.603688712s selected gray wolf after gray wolf had been defeated, at population 1.02. The old matching rule accepted `灰狼` as a substring inside `傷痕灰狼`, so the generic row could win over the specific goal. This is a QA policy finding, not evidence of a product goal-selection defect. The original artifact does not store the exact goal/eligible list on each TRACK. The corrected retry records them directly and chooses the specific scarred-wolf row when the goal says `追蹤傷痕灰狼`; its evidence replaces the old “only gray” inference. The normal-save population block remains a separate product pacing observation.

## §61 question 3: do items create meaningfully different builds?

**Systemic differentiation is present; normal-play strategic build separation remains unproven.** UI and actual drops expose attack, defense/block, critical, penetration and affix differences. Controlled simulations show actual outcome differences: Defense upgraded versus legacy in 43/96 Edge and 49/96 Ready cells, with 16 and 14 tradeoffs respectively. Penetration had a limited advantage over its same-tier bleed control in the paired matrix, but no strict separation against the controlled armored elite at Edge/Ready. Mixed Crit/Bleed/Defense profiles change multiple variables and cannot isolate trait causality; high-power win ceilings also compress outcomes. The Adventure helper equipped and compared varied items but its automatic positive-stat rule repeatedly oscillated between candidates (including item-3/item-14 armor), which is a harness limitation rather than evidence of product-induced player labor.

Static weighted scores, `rawBelowLegacy`, and rarity/profile screens are not ECV or observed player value. The ECV-like evidence here is limited to the declared Pareto comparison of actual engine outcomes under sampled scenarios; it is not a complete economic, progression or human-choice measure.
