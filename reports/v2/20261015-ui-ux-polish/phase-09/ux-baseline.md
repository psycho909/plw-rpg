## Prioritized findings

### P2 · P9-02 — Boss preparation gives rank and eligibility, but no explicit danger or retreat/cooldown summary

Before entering combat, the forest view shows the wolf-track rank, unmet eligibility reasons, reward expectations, and a next adventure goal. The ordinary Goblin Chief panel also identifies the challenge. It does not provide an explicit danger estimate/recommended preparation level or a compact explanation of retreat and cooldown rules. Do not add a numeric estimate unless the combat model supports one; a short qualitative summary grounded in existing traits and rules would close the information gap.

### P2 · P9-01 / P9-05 — Main HUD controls fall below a 1000 px viewport

The clipping also affects common laptop viewport heights. At 1280×768 the player identity begins at y=747 and is clipped by the bottom; recent event and shortcuts begin at y=798, fully below the viewport. At 1280×900 identity begins at y=879 and is clipped, while recent event and shortcuts begin at y=930. At 1280×1000 identity begins at y=979 and bottom controls at y=1030. At 320×1000 and 375×1000 all four mobile navigation buttons begin at y=1016. Across widths 320, 375, 768, 1280, 1920 and heights 768, 900, 1000, there was no horizontal overflow; sampled targets were at least 24×24. The 375 px inventory dialog fit inside the viewport and its body did not overflow. Consider reclaiming vertical space from the map or arranging the persistent HUD/navigation so primary status and controls fit in the initial viewport.

### P3 · P9-04 — Generic monster loot and recipe-registered materials are hard to connect

The Hybrid route won an encounter and showed one generic monster material in inventory. The `素材與收成` tab had no discovered registered crafting material. Searching the tree-resin armor recipe showed Smithing Lv.2 required, three slime resin required / zero held, insufficient wooden material, and that the station was not open; the craft button was disabled with these visible constraints. This is a discoverability candidate, not an unexplained disabled action or a failed craft. Consider labeling generic loot separately and exposing the link from collected drops to registered recipe materials and their sources.

Evidence: [adventure-loot-comparison.png](a-runner/attempt-20261015T082613437Z-ux-audit/adventure-loot-comparison.png), [inventory-after-craft.png](a-runner/attempt-20261015T082613437Z-ux-audit/inventory-after-craft.png), final `results.json` fields `adventure.hybrid` and `life.craftPlan`. ### P9-03 controlled-cap evaluation — explicit feedback; no new defect evidenced

At practice cap the workbench explicitly identifies the cap, disables crafting when the fixture has no materials, states the material blocker, and presents the next recipe target. Selecting an unlocked advanced recipe likewise shows required/current quantities and an inline materials warning; its recipe selector remains enabled while the craft action is disabled. This fixture does not support a finding that capped XP is hidden or that blockers are only represented by disabled controls. Keep Phase8 E-F3's conditional chore-risk signal separate: at the observed zero-XP timestamps no alternate legal XP recipe was projected, although trade was available.

## What already works

- Life actions state stamina costs and gather/craft feedback; the craft plan connects held and required resources, time, fee, skill requirement, XP cap, quality floor, and next unlock.
- Inventory provides equipment comparison and an explicit equip action; character view confirms the resulting skill state.
- Adventure shows rank/eligibility and reward expectations before encounter, then displays victory reward, loot, and a next goal.
- The crisis panel lays out current needs and support costs, confirms contributions, and presents an outcome. This was exercised only with the labeled fixture.
- Searchable recipe results, visible requirement explanations, modal focus handling, and touch-sized mobile inventory navigation worked in this run.
